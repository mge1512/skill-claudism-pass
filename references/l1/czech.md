# Czech first language

Read `common.md` first. Much of this applies to Slovak and Polish as well.

## Articles

Czech has no articles, and this is the most visible interference in English.
English needs one before almost every singular countable noun.

- First mention of something countable: "a build service".
- Later mentions, or something already identified: "the build service".
- No article for uncountable nouns and general plurals: "software", "packages
  are signed".
- No article before most proper names: "openSUSE released", not "the openSUSE
  released".

When unsure, read the sentence aloud and ask whether the reader already knows
which one is meant. If yes, "the". If no, "a".

## Tense and aspect

Czech marks aspect on the verb, English marks it with tense forms.

- "I work here since 2019" -> "I have worked here since 2019".
- "I am working on it yesterday" -> "I worked on it yesterday".
- "I will send it when I will finish" -> "when I finish".

## False friends

| Written | Means in Czech | English intended |
| --- | --- | --- |
| actual | aktuální | current |
| realize | realizovat | carry out, implement |
| control | kontrolovat | check |
| concrete | konkrétní | specific |
| eventually | eventuálně | possibly |
| sympathetic | sympatický | likeable |
| novel | novela | amendment (to a law) |
| prospect | prospekt | brochure |
| billion | bilion | trillion |
| host | hosté | guests |
| possibility | možnost | opportunity |

"We realized the migration" reads in English as "we became aware of the
migration". Use "carried out" or "completed".

## Other

- Double negation is required in Czech and wrong in English: "I do not know
  anything", not "I do not know nothing".
- Prepositions: "at the university", not "on the university".
- "resp." (respektive) is as common in Czech as in German. See `common.md`.
