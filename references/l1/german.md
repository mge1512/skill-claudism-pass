Read `common.md` first. This file adds what is specific to German speakers.

## Structure

- **Nominalstil**. "The implementation of the migration of the configuration"
  -> "migrating the configuration". German rewards nouns, English rewards verbs.
  This is the single largest readability gain in most German-written English.
- Long subordinate chains. Split at the point where German would put the verb.
- "How does it look like" (wie sieht es aus). See `common.md`.
- Comma before "that": German sets one before the relative clause, English does
  not. "I think, that this works" -> "I think that this works".
- Capitalised nouns mid-sentence.
- "We have here a problem" -> "We have a problem here".
- After the greeting, German continues in lower case ("Hallo zusammen," then
  "nach der Diskussion..."). English capitalises the first word of the next
  sentence.
- "help me generating" -> "help me generate". German takes the infinitive with
  "zu"; English takes the bare infinitive after "help".

## Register

German technical writing is more formal than English technical writing. "Sehr
geehrte Damen und Herren" becomes "Hello" or "Hi" in most internal and partner
mail, not "Dear Sirs". Passive voice is heavier in German; prefer the active in
English.
