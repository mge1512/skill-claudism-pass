# Greek first language

Read `common.md` first.

## False friends

| Written | Means in Greek | English intended |
| --- | --- | --- |
| empathy | εμπάθεια | animosity, prejudice |
| idiotic | ιδιωτικός | private |
| sympathetic | συμπαθητικός | nice, likeable |
| pathetic | παθητικός | passive |
| eventually | ενδεχομένως | possibly |
| actual | επίκαιρος | current |
| dramatic | δραματικός | theatrical |
| billion | δισεκατομμύριο | 10^9, unlike the older long-scale reading |

"Empathy" is the sharpest of these: the Greek word means the opposite of the
English one, so a sentence praising someone's empathy can read as an accusation
to a Greek reader and vice versa.

## Articles

Greek uses the definite article where English does not: before abstract nouns,
before proper names, and before generic plurals.

- "The life is hard" -> "life is hard".
- "The John will review it" -> "John will review it".
- "The packages are signed" only when specific packages are meant; otherwise
  "packages are signed".

## Tense

- "I am here since 2019" -> "I have been here since 2019".
- The aorist covers ground that English splits between the past simple and the
  present perfect. "I already sent it" -> "I have already sent it" when the
  result still stands.

## Names and transliteration

Greek names reach Latin script in several forms (Georgios, Giorgos, Yorgos).
Pick one spelling per document and keep it, including in mail addresses and
author lines.
