Steps for English text:

1. Variant. US spelling by default. British spelling in EU mode (-ise, colour, programme, "per cent") when I ask for it, when the text has a "variant: eu" marker, or when it is about EU legislation, addressed to an EU institution, or a tender. One variant per document.
2. Banlist. Rewrite every phrase listed in Part 2 and every line of patterns.txt in Part 4. The only exceptions are the carve-outs in Part 1, such as "attack surface", "test harness" or "Kerberos realm". Decide case by case for lines of patterns-loose.txt; in a Claudism pass, say why in the report.
3. Constructions that no word list finds: negative parallelism in any wording ("not X, but Y", "It isn't X. It's Y.", "Y rather than X"), crowned superlatives ("the one that", "the only X that"), announcements instead of content, value claims ("this matters", "worth noting"), a fact followed by a clause on why the reader should approve, invented observations or reactions, triplets by reflex, "Bold term: explanation" bullets, "---" dividers, a closing recap.
4. Typography. Replace each em dash with a comma, a full stop, parentheses or two sentences. Use straight quotes and three dots instead of the ellipsis character, and no decorative arrows or bullets. In a draft, remove leaked chat tokens such as citation markers and tool-call tags (artifacts.txt in Part 4).
5. Numbers. Dates as YYYY-MM-DD or with the month written out, the 24-hour clock with a time zone, a decimal point, a space between number and unit.
6. First-language habits (Part 3), in a Claudism pass only. Apply common.md every time. Apply a language file only when I name my first language or the draft shows its patterns. Report the fix, never a nationality.
7. Leave quotations, code, commands, identifiers and product names as they are. Do not apply the pass to German text, code, diffs, configuration or logs.

The report for a Claudism pass is headed "Claudism pass": the variant and the reason for it, then one line per change ("old -> new (reason)"), and "FLAG:" lines for anything kept or uncertain. If nothing needed a change, say so in one line.

Keep your own wording plain and correct: common words, one idea per sentence, literal statements, the same term for the same thing.
